How we will work with these questions

  • Prepare brief notes on all four questions before class — a few bullet points each is enough.
  • In class we discuss them in the order below, in small groups, then share with the room.
  • Bring evidence from the case (and your own examples) — not just opinions.
  • Use the frameworks from our sessions: culture (Hall, Hofstede, Meyer), the three Ms, crisis management and SCCT.
  • There are no single right answers — you are graded on the quality of your reasoning.

Be ready to take and defend a position — especially on whether the Shanghai show should have gone ahead.

Why did the campaign offend?

Discussion question 1

  • What were the intended “jokes” in the three videos?
  • Why did most Chinese — and many other — viewers receive them so negatively?
  • Which specific cultural cues were misread, and why do they matter?
  • Could the same idea have been advertised in a better, more effective way?

Prepare with: the case narrative; the culture session (Hall, Hofstede, Meyer); the idea of high- vs. low-context cultures.

Humour and the brand

Discussion question 2

  • How does humour fit — or clash with — D&G’s luxury brand image?
  • What are the advantages of humorous appeals in advertising?
  • What are the risks, especially when an ad crosses cultures?
  • How would you “turn it around” to get the intended humorous effect?

Prepare with: the three Ms of offensiveness (matter, media, manner); the #DGLovesJapan contrast.

Crisis communication

Discussion question 3

  • How effective was D&G’s crisis response — what did it do, and fail to do?
  • What should it have done, and how quickly?
  • How should social media change the way a company responds?
  • How could D&G prevent a recurrence in China and other markets?

Prepare with: the three phases of crisis management; “stealing thunder”; attribution theory / SCCT.

Repairing and rebuilding in China

Discussion question 4

  • How can D&G repair the immediate reputational damage?
  • How can it rebuild its long-term image in China?
  • Who should be involved — local teams, agencies, partners, government?
  • What would signal genuine change rather than a quick PR patch?

Prepare with: the reputation-repair strategies; the two HBR readings on preparing for and recovering from faux pas.

Take it further — your own market entry

  • Look at your market-entry group assignment: where could your MNE make a cultural misstep in its target market?
  • Name one realistic faux pas — in advertising, product, or management — and how the local audience might read it.
  • Add two safeguards (e.g. local review, testing, empowered local staff) to your risk-management analysis.

The D&G case is a warning; your job is to design the entry so it does not become your case.
