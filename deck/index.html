Aim of the course

The international expansion of their operations is central for the survival of an increasing number of firms. In a context of deglobalization, nationalism, and geo-political tensions, this internationalization poses novel challenges for firms and requires them to rethink their existing strategy.

In this module we analyze

  • The strategies used when expanding overseas into new country markets
  • The strategies used to operate and develop an existing network of international operations
  • How firms adapt these strategies to a context of deglobalization, nationalism, and geo-political tensions

Learning objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, you will:

  • Have greater awareness of the deglobalization, nationalism, and geo-political tensions characterizing the current business environment
  • Become aware of the various types of international strategies available to internationally operating firms
  • Recognize how particular characteristics of firms and their environments affect the choice and implementation of international business strategies
  • Develop the skills needed for solving the challenges firms face when considering different international business strategies in a dynamic environment
  • Demonstrate improved critical thinking and problem-solving skills
  • Improve communication skills necessary for effective team performance

Teaching methods

This is a highly interactive course. Learning is maximized by balancing lecture with your involvement in discussions, cases, and exercises. Students are expected to be fully engaged and to participate in classroom discussions. All course documents, assignments and lecture notes are posted online.

The course is comprised of

  • Lectures
  • In-class case analyses
  • A team research project
  • Teamwork reflection
  • A final team presentation

Pre-course assignment

A concise summary of the historical development of the international operations of a company:

  • Select an internationally operating company of your choice — more information is available on large, publicly listed (e.g. S&P 500) firms
  • Write a short report (max. 1,000 words) on the firm's international operations: which country markets, when it entered or left them, and how (e.g. establishing a subsidiary)
  • Explain why the firm expanded to those markets and chose those entry modes
  • Start from the firm's website and annual reports; complement with newspaper reports — no academic journals required

Course literature

Course materials and cases are provided online. No specific textbook is required — a general International Business textbook provides useful background reading, for example:

  • Charles W. Hill — Competing in the Global Marketplace (various editions)
  • Daniels, Radebaugh & Sullivan — International Business (various editions)

Selected journal articles complement the class discussions, including work by Barkema et al. (1996), Dunning, Delios & Henisz (2003), Dhanaraj & Beamish (2004), Hofstede (1980) and Inkpen & Beamish (1997), among others. The full reading list is provided in the course outline.
